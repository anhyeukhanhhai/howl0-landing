# howl0 repositioning brief

Version 1.0 · 21 September 2026  
Audience: founders, brand, product, marketing and development teams

## 1. Purpose of this revision

This is an evolution of the current howl0 strategy, not a restart.

The previous website led with a useful but narrow product story: make music-homework submission easier through one simple link. That remains an important product advantage, but it does not fully express the larger problem howl0 is now exploring.

The new strategic centre is:

> Make the learning between lessons visible, connected and easier to act on.

howl0 should connect the practice loop across three people:

- the teacher who sets direction and gives feedback;
- the student who practises and improves;
- the parent or guardian who wants to understand progress and offer appropriate support.

The submission link remains the easiest way into the loop. It is the entry mechanism, not the complete brand promise.

## 2. Evidence discipline

### Founder-confirmed direction

- howl0 serves three central audiences: teachers, students and parents or guardians.
- the initial context is music learning between lessons, beginning with homework, audio or video submissions and teacher feedback.
- the teacher remains responsible for learning direction and feedback.
- students are the main everyday learners and users.
- parents need an appropriate view of progress, particularly for younger learners.
- the simple submission link remains a key part of the product experience.
- Vietnam is the priority research market; Australia remains relevant.
- howl0 is currently in development.

### Existing early signals

- some teachers currently manage homework through fragmented channels such as Zalo, Messenger, WhatsApp, email and shared drives.
- teachers have reported problems including misplaced files, inconsistent naming, forgotten submissions and time spent finding work.
- teachers want convenience, reliability, accessibility and an attractive, easy-to-use experience.

These signals are early and limited. They do not prove market size, willingness to pay or repeat product adoption.

### Current hypotheses requiring validation

- parents in Vietnam lack a useful way to understand how their child is progressing between lessons.
- parents feel a stronger pain than teachers or students and may have the highest willingness to pay.
- parents value evidence of effort, feedback and improvement—not only final performances or exam results.
- teachers can act as the main adoption and distribution channel.
- teachers will consistently use the product if parent visibility is part of the value proposition.
- parents will register, use or pay for the product only when their child's teacher participates.
- a Vietnamese-first or bilingual experience materially improves adoption.
- the market lacks a suitable existing solution for continuous music-practice progress.
- the most valuable progress information is practice frequency, submitted attempts, teacher feedback, next steps and change over time.

Do not present these hypotheses as established facts on the website, in sales materials or in product descriptions.

## 3. Reframed problem

Music learning does not happen only during a lesson. Most improvement happens between lessons, but that period is difficult to see and keep connected.

A common learning flow is:

1. the teacher explains a task;
2. the student practises at home;
3. the student sends a recording;
4. the teacher gives limited feedback;
5. the student attempts the task again;
6. the next lesson becomes the first time everyone can clearly see whether the guidance worked.

When that loop is fragmented:

- teachers cannot easily maintain a continuous record of attempts and feedback;
- students may not understand what changed or what to practise next;
- parents see the result but not the learning process behind it.

The opportunity is not to score children more aggressively or increase surveillance. It is to give each authorised person an appropriate, understandable view of the same learning journey.

## 4. Repositioning

### Recommended category

**Music learning progress platform**

This is more specific and meaningful than a generic LMS, but broad enough to grow beyond homework submission.

### Entry wedge

**A simple link for submitting music practice.**

The link removes friction and introduces the wider progress loop.

### Continuing value

**A connected record of practice, teacher feedback and next steps over time.**

### One-line description

> howl0 connects practice, teacher feedback and the next step, so students, teachers and parents can understand progress between lessons.

### Positioning statement

For music teachers, learners and families who struggle to see what happens between lessons, howl0 is a music learning progress platform that connects assignments, practice recordings, teacher feedback and next steps in one continuous loop. Unlike scattered chat threads, file folders or generic learning systems, howl0 begins with a simple submission link and builds an understandable record of progress around the teacher's guidance.

### Brand promise

> Make progress visible, one practice loop at a time.

### Core belief to retain

> Feedback is a signal, not a verdict.

This belief remains highly relevant. The product should show feedback as part of an ongoing loop, not as a permanent judgement or public score.

## 5. Audience architecture

| Audience | Role in the system | Primary tension | Desired value | Hypothesis to test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teacher | Adoption gatekeeper, learning authority and likely distribution channel | Repeated homework and feedback are difficult to organise and follow over time | Less administration, clearer continuity and a reliable record of guidance | Will teachers adopt consistently, recommend it and pay or support parent payment? |
| Student | Everyday user and primary beneficiary | Feedback can feel disconnected, unclear or judgemental | A simple submission experience, understandable next steps and visible progress | Does visibility motivate practice without adding pressure? |
| Parent or guardian | Trust stakeholder and potential economic buyer | Sees outcomes but may not understand effort, feedback or development between lessons | A calm, contextual view of progress and a better way to support the child | Is this pain frequent and strong enough to drive registration or payment? |

### Important commercial distinction

The person with the strongest emotional pain may not be the first person who can successfully adopt the product.

The working commercial hypothesis is:

> Teacher-led adoption, student-led usage and parent-supported value or payment.

This is not yet a confirmed business model. Research must test:

- who first agrees to use howl0;
- who performs setup and maintains the workflow;
- who receives enough recurring value to pay;
- whether parents will pay without teacher participation;
- whether teachers need an incentive, revenue share, discount or other reason to introduce the product;
- whether direct parent acquisition creates demand or creates friction with teachers.

## 6. Messaging hierarchy

### Level 1 — Core outcome

**See the progress between lessons.**

### Level 2 — How howl0 creates that outcome

Assignments, practice recordings, feedback and next steps remain connected in one learning loop.

### Level 3 — Easy entry

Students begin through one simple submission link, without having to learn a complicated platform first.

### Level 4 — Role-specific value

- Teachers guide continuously and keep learning evidence organised.
- Students understand what changed and what to try next.
- Parents follow progress with context, without taking over the teacher's role.

### Level 5 — Trust boundary

The teacher remains responsible for learning guidance. Parent access is authorised and age-appropriate. Future AI may assist but must not replace teacher judgement.

## 7. Recommended messages by audience

### Teacher

**Headline:** Keep every attempt connected to the next step.  
**Message:** Assign work, review recordings and carry feedback forward without searching across disconnected chats and folders.

### Student

**Headline:** Know what to practise next.  
**Message:** Submit your recording easily, understand your teacher's feedback and see how each attempt moves you forward.

### Parent or guardian

**Headline:** See the learning behind the result.  
**Message:** Follow your child's practice journey with useful context from their teacher—not only the final performance.

### Partner or investor

**Headline:** A shared progress layer for music learning between lessons.  
**Message:** howl0 begins with low-friction practice submission and aims to connect the teacher, student and family around an ongoing record of guidance and progress.

## 8. Product-story boundaries

### Emphasise

- progress between lessons;
- continuity from one attempt to the next;
- teacher-led guidance;
- student clarity and confidence;
- appropriate parent visibility;
- one-link submission as the easy starting point;
- bilingual accessibility for Vietnam and Australia;
- privacy, authorised access and age-appropriate language.

### Avoid

- claiming that parents are confirmed buyers;
- stating that no competitor exists in Vietnam;
- promising measurable musical improvement before evidence exists;
- framing parent access as surveillance or control;
- public scores, rankings or comparisons between children;
- describing howl0 as a finished platform;
- inventing pricing, launch dates, paid plans or AI capabilities;
- positioning howl0 as a teacher replacement;
- reducing the product to file upload alone;
- using generic LMS language as the leading message.

## 9. Brand voice

The voice should be:

- clear, warm and concise;
- professional enough for teachers and parents;
- encouraging without sounding childish;
- emotionally intelligent about learning and feedback;
- confident about the direction but honest about development status;
- natural in both English and Vietnamese.

Use short sentences and active language. Prefer "follow progress" over "monitor performance." Prefer "understand the learning journey" over "control practice." Avoid educational bureaucracy and inflated startup language.

## 10. Bilingual transcreation principles

Vietnamese copy must be written for Vietnamese readers, not translated word by word.

- Preserve the intent and emotional effect rather than English syntax.
- Use natural education language such as `theo sát quá trình`, `hiểu con đang tiến bộ ra sao`, `biết mình cần luyện gì tiếp theo` and `góp ý của giáo viên`.
- Avoid stiff phrases such as `giám sát hiệu suất`, `nền tảng quản trị học tập toàn diện` or literal translations of idioms.
- Keep sentences compact enough for the existing interface.
- Do not translate the brand name `howl0`.
- Translate calls to action by purpose: `Join the waitlist` becomes `Đăng ký nhận tin`, not the mechanical `Tham gia danh sách chờ`.
- The two versions may use different sentence structures when needed, but they must make the same promise and respect the same evidence limits.

## 11. Website objective

The website should make a visitor understand three things quickly:

1. howl0 is about progress between music lessons;
2. it connects teacher guidance, student practice and parent understanding;
3. it begins with a simple submission link and is still being developed.

The current visual system and interaction design can remain. Copy, labels, information hierarchy and selected diagrams should change so the website tells this new story.

## 12. Validation questions that branding must not answer prematurely

- Which audience experiences the strongest recurring pain?
- Who initiates adoption?
- Who performs setup?
- Who pays first?
- Will parents pay if the teacher does not actively use the product?
- What progress information do parents want, and what would feel intrusive?
- What progress information do teachers feel comfortable sharing?
- Does visible progress motivate students or create pressure?
- Which Vietnamese wording feels supportive rather than controlling?
- Does a Vietnamese-language experience materially increase trust and completion?

Website analytics, waitlist responses, interviews, the questionnaire and MVP behaviour should be used together to answer these questions.
